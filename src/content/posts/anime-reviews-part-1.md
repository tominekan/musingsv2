I think I’ll start giving my thoughts on the the most recent anime I’ve watched (or that I’ve been thinking about). These shows are listed in no particular order. Since this is the first edition, I’ll note my thoughts on anime in general. 

I’m a pretty simple man. I prefer an action packed anime with a decent plot (or at least one that isn’t so basic that it gets in the way of the story). This does mean that I’m a sucker for good fights and main character (MC) aura farming scenes. Also for some weird reason, I can never get myself to sit down and watch a show focused on mechas.

### Female characters in Anime

Before I get to the story, I need to vent about a terrible problem that anime suffers from.

This is an issue that I feel so strongly about that I need to dedicate a whole section of this article to rant. One common thread across _a lot_ of shows is that women are reduced to eyecandy that are either damsels in distress, hopeless romantics, or saints with no common sense. Now I’m not some gung-ho feminist (or a mysogynist pls), but it genuinely pains me to see a woman who has so much potential within a story get flattened into a one-dimensional mold for no obvious reason. Like, you talk to a woman in reality, and you see the stuff in anime, and you’re just wondering just what kind of women these authors are talking to.

An annoying amount of shows will _tell_ you about how this woman is super powerful and whatnot, and you’ll _watch_ her do nothing but rely on the MC (and usually, *he* ain’t shit). It’s even more annoying when they have one moment that truly shows the kind of character they’re capable of becoming, and we just _never_ see that again (looking at you Naruto). 

Another problem I have is that a lot of the interactions between the MC’s and women are just plain weird. Like I can’t begin to imagine that a well-adjusted person would sit down and draw out scenes of these often dubiously aged girls being borderline sexually harassed by grown ass men (looking at Jobless Reincarnation), and usually these girls are “enjoying it”. _What?_ These authors are some low-key freaks, and not the good kind.

As a side note, especially in the less popular shows, the high pitched voice acting tends to annoy the shit out of me. 

With that out of my system, let’s get to the acutal reviews.

###  1. Attack on Titan
Honestly I didn’t want to watch this anime for a while because I found the first episode pretty boring like 2 years go. However, I recently gave it a second try, and after slogging through first episode, the rest is peak fiction. It’s also one of the few shows where I can remember the characters names (a hallmark of a fantastic show). Naturally, I have a lot to say about this.

One thing that the show does really well with with is showing just how horrific war truly is. You’ll often see shows that ignore this aspect to focus on the overarching conflict between good and evil. However, Attack on Titan throws that aside, quickly makes clear that there is no “good” in war, only evils, and even victory feels like utter loss.

Another thing that’s amazing about this show are the female characters. I think they might be the most well-written, fleshed-out women in all the anime I’ve watched. They get the same attention is the male characters, and it really shows. Even Mikasa, often cited as a character solely focused on Eren clearly is well thought out. She’s keenly aware of her inability to truly hurt Eren. However, since the beginning of the story, shes never been afraid to call him out when he goes on his usual BS. Plus, she’s just a badass in general.

The visuals are stunning, the voice acting is great, and the story is powerful. The only thing that holds it back was that in the final season, I kind of felt like the world expanded waaay to fast, with the Marleyan vs Titan conflict being shoved in my face.

### 2. Ishura
Uhh, I’m still questioning who the main character is in this show. Ishura has good visuals, great fight scenes, and a pretty tangled but interesting political climate. One thing I appreciate is how fucked up almost every single single character is. Their world is not a kind or simple place, and each character is accordingly complex. This show also doesn’t suffer from weak female characters, so that’s always a bonus.

I think the most glaring flaw in this show is that there’s no main character/cast that we’re supposed to really care about and watch develop throughout the story. Each episode is an exploration into another facet of the world the characters reside in. While this is great for world-building, it’s pretty bad for keeping an audience invested in the story. You could likely skip a few episodes and not feel that something’s amiss. With that being said, I’m still a sucker for good fights and I’ll be waiting to watch season 3.

### 3. Berserk of Gluttony
This is your standard, run of the mill anime. You have a weak, impoverished character with a weird but overpowered skill, they’re doted upon by {insert female love interest}, they unlock their skill and get super strong, they defeat some big-bad very clearly evil villain, and voila, we have a cookie-cutter anime. The animation was okay, the voice acting was alright, I’m honestly not too sure why I watched an entire season of this. Safe to say I won’t be watching any more.

### 4. BUNGO Stray Dogs
This is also a pretty generic show, but it’s executed pretty well. The core cast of characters are genuinely lovable, and the author really takes the time to develop the relationship between these characters so that the lengths in which they go to protect each other in important moments doesn’t seem obviously unreasonable. The animation is pretty solid, and the fights are pretty meaningful. The one thing that kind of confuses me is why tiger-boy’s heaing factor works in some fights but not others.

To be honest though, I was coding for the majority of the time that I was watching this anime, so it seems less interesting than it would’ve been if I dedicated my time to fully watching this. I only watched like 4 of the 5 seasons and I’m kind of not interested in watching the fifth.

### 5. The Disastrous Life of Saiki K
Normally, I don’t watch slice of life type shows, but this one actually got me hooked. It’s super funny and it has a cast of genuinely lovable characters. We can see that Saiki, even though he’s super powerful and largely mature, is still a high-school (or is it middle school) aged kid at his core. The animation is nothing crazy, but the again, it doesn’t need to be.

The defining feature of this show has to be the cast though, you really get to fall in love with these characters (Nendo has to be the funniest character of all time), as they go through everyday situations and I was honestly sad when I finished both seasons. I just found out there’s another season, “The Disastrous Life of Saiki K: Reawakened”, so I’ll definitely be watching that soon.

I didn’t talk about the tons of anime I sort of watched but didn’t enjoy, but I’ll try to get to a few in later editions of this series. I think in the next one I’ll talk about my favorite anime of all time, “One Piece.”
